# Video-call proof before commercial launch

The founder's latest September 9 instruction selects **PWA first**, targeting comparable foreground calling and an eventual lawful consenting-adult explicit service. This supersedes Apple-native-first planning. Responsive, realistic video remains the main technical test. The running demo is neutral; adult capability, hosting/processor approval and real iPhone performance remain unproven.

## What runs

The existing RTX 4060 Ti 16GB / 48GB RAM PC runs one local companion. Text, Voice call and Video call share memory. Calls use microphone input, can deliver a separate message to Text, and continue across tab changes. Call controls are mute/end icons; the composer stays in Text. Cloudflare plans replies; CPU Kokoro/Whisper and GPU LTX/MuseTalk produce media. No camera, signup, checkout or public deployment.

The shared flow lives in `local_app/engine.py`. Private `.env` contains implemented startup choices. [LOCAL_POC](LOCAL_POC.md) is the technical source for models, reproductions and measurements.

## Evidence and gaps

- Direct body generation took 4.50s at 384x576, 3.19s at 320x480 and 2.00s at 256x384 in warm samples. The fastest moved both hands; these times exclude the rest of the call.
- Reviewed approach → greeting → return began browser playback in **1.72s / 2.16s / 1.83s**, with zero stalls and completed unmuted audio. The final-build approach/return check took **2.61s / 2.68s**, also with no stalls. Prepared transitions now survive intervening conversation, with a blinking listening loop at both full-body and close positions.
- New speech and lips are generated over prepared footage. A bounded source-appearance cache reduces repeated tracking/encoding; long replies loop the body instead of freezing. The scene guard now prevents garden descriptions from discarding a close view.
- A **13.092s** spoken count took **9.97s to start / 23.489s server completion**, with two stalls before caching. A warm-cache repeat took **9.54s / 19.680s**, with no stalls; rendering improved from 15.943s to 11.182s. That starting delay remains unacceptable for a call. Another 6.155s reply began in 4.23s with no stalls. These are samples, not p95 or universal two-second latency.
- LTX-2.3 successfully prepared the approach in 146.429s cold. Several close-idle prompts failed review before a blink-only guided clip worked. Its fresh joint audio/video remains too slow for live replacement; arbitrary LTX-2B commands still fail direction/framing. Prepared motion is disclosed.
- **124 Python / 16 Node checks pass**, including GPU-session cost, iPhone streaming selection, silent-gesture audio completion, bounded/cancelled priming, complete phrase playback, displayed-pose cancellation, voice interruption and stale-capture rejection. Synthetic Chromium media checks cover captions, call/Text navigation, unmuted audio completion and ending calls. Unmuted browser completion is not confirmation of physical speaker output.
- The latest UI gives the character 854px of height at the 716x854 preview, up from 544px. Side controls, compact tabs and phone framing retain head and feet. Five synthetic Chromium viewport checks passed, including microphone denial and call/Text navigation; the live desktop preview was also visually reviewed.
- The reviewed retimed idle is now active: about 59% less measured side-background motion and a roughly 0.25s bilateral blink. Minor morphing/seam movement and repetition remain. Short ambient replies now end with speech: a 1.035s greeting no longer forces 5.25s of reply playback.
- Six synthetic [Cloudflare Aura-2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/aura-2-en/) speech calls delivered their first 4KB in 0.278–0.395s, but complete long WAVs took 6.014–7.264s. The existing renderer needs the complete speech track, so switching providers alone would not solve long-reply latency. Kokoro remains selected; nominal benchmark spend was about $0.018 including one initial parser failure, before any free allocation.
- The follow-up Cloudflare comparison favors keeping Qwen (0.455–0.771s) over Granite (1.289–2.108s) for the tested dialogue path. GLM-4.7-Flash exhausted the adapter's output limit, then timed out. Same-text long speech took 2.153s on local Kokoro, 2.435s on Aura-1, 7.269s on Aura-2 and 2.005s on Melo. ASR flags and exact prices are in LOCAL_POC; no blanket voice-quality winner is claimed. The unsupported-cartwheel reply was misleading and is corrected in the selected Qwen prompt.
- FlashHead Lite produced 0.96s chunks in about 0.79–0.88s after startup on this GPU, using about 5GB. A 30.72s speech-plus-silence follow-up took 25.592s to generate/encode, with identity retained in sampled frames. Close-up speech visibly moves the mouth/head; full-body mouth motion is weak. It is an isolated research candidate, not the app renderer or an arbitrary-body-action solution.

Longer replies now retain visual motion, but short loops repeat and approach frames have visible blur. Reliable arbitrary motion, browser-close recovery, identity/lips, acoustic device testing and sustained p95 latency remain open. This is not production-ready.

The new phrase pipeline reduces first playback from **4.10s to 2.33s** in a matched synthetic-planner/ASR count comparison. Real speech, GPU rendering and browser playback completed; all words were recovered by ASR. The requested approach happened once and subsequent phrases stayed close. There are still **0.58–0.99s inter-phrase gaps** in those samples. Batch 16 introduced stalls and was rejected. These measurements exclude real ASR/dialogue and do not establish universal two-second calls; LOCAL_POC records the full comparison and remaining visual defects.

Interrupt now preserves the displayed pose instead of the generated endpoint. Two real browser trials stopped an approach at 0.8s / 1.6s, saved the pose in **136ms / 94ms**, spoke from it, then returned or continued using the remaining reviewed footage. The second trial's next speech / movement started in **1.46s / 1.84s**, excluding real recognition and planning. This improves continuity; it does not fix the source footage's blur or establish arbitrary real-time body generation.
